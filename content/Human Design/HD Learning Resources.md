## Everything I write about Human Design comes from *my* understanding of the system.
I honestly think this is the only way it works: learning the core mechanics can give you a sense of the themes that tend to hold true for everybody, but the *way* those mechanics play out in any one chart is very individualized. So, when I write about a channel or gate that's defined in my own chart, my perspective is really coming from a combination of factors through my entire definition, and through the context of my own lived experience. The same gate or channel defined in your chart may not feel relatable in the same way, but there will be some kind of shared thread within its broader theme. This is the kind of work that a professional reading with someone looking at your whole chart can help with, but in the end it's always down to **you** to feel when something lands or doesn't.

### A selection of free resources to explore
I've become very interested in the mechanics and am learning from all sorts of sources: I actually think having multiple sources makes for a much richer experience and greater depth of understanding, which is why I'm not too picky about whether a resource is teaching things in the "official" International Human Design School style.

While I can write all day about my own knowledge (which is always evolving and deepening), I'm not interested in starting yet another HD resource site. If the premise of Human Design piques your interest, you can generate a chart of your own for free just about anywhere as long as you know your exact birth time: most links below will have a chart generator but my suggestion is [bodygraph.io](https://bodygraph.io/?r=2ATPFRF). If you're curious to learn even more, I'm happy to share some of my top picks for gathering **free** info, which in turn can give you an idea of the kinds of paid resources (including personalized chart readings) you might be interested in. See for yourself if any of these speaks to you:

#### Understanding the Basics: Type, Strategy, Authority, Profile through an experiential lens

> [!info]- [Moonrise](https://www.youtube.com/@moonrisecommunity)
>  My personal recommendation for newcomers who want to really get a feel for what Human Design is all about is to generate your chart somewhere and then check out the Moonrise videos for [your particular Type](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PL2cwQX9l95j71p3gMiKoCWxBEese80HcG).
>  
> While unfortunately no longer making content under the Moonrise banner, Sam Pfotenhauer and Nadia Last have some great beginner-friendly resources in the forms of [Youtube videos](https://www.youtube.com/@moonrisecommunity) and a [podcast](https://open.spotify.com/show/2xII4UkLhQmBKx0fk7ID7K). They approach Human Design as a system of self-love and self-understanding, which is so relatable to me. Their content hits a sweet spot of being grounded in foundational Human Design knowledge and speaking to their audience from an experiential place, which makes everything they talk about feel very easy to conceptualize and relate to. Sam's and Nadia's designs are quite different from each other, giving the pair of them pretty good firsthand coverage of what it feels like to have a center defined or undefined, or using very different types of strategies and authorities. I *love* watching and hearing these two talk Human Design together: their friendship with each other radiates through the way they communicate and brings so much energy, warmth, understanding, and curiosity to the topics. You can really tell that while they worked on the Moonrise project together, they were both coming at it from a place of real passion. Watching a video always leaves me with a smile on my face. The only downside to checking out Moonrise is that you may end up wanting more than they have in their archive!
> 
> Though Moonrise is no longer active (making their old recorded promotions of their training course feel a bit like a gut punch), both [Nadia](https://www.nadialast.com/) and [Sam](https://www.wildriver.live/) are still giving readings through their own individual business offerings.

> [!info]- [Maggie Ostara of Sovereignty by Design](https://sovereigntybydesign.com/)
> Of all the influences I've found useful, Maggie Ostara is probably the most spiritually-oriented in a way that can sometimes feel challenging for me, which is not a bad thing. I discovered her on [Youtube](https://www.youtube.com/maggieostarahumandesignfemininesovereignty), where she has a ton of fundamental HD learning videos as well as a more in-depth collection focusing on embodied HD authority (a key concept) and videos on upcoming planetary transits through a Human Design lens. Deep in her video library are some longer recordings of live sessions where she speed-reads charts, which the budding reader in me found particularly interesting.
> 
> Maggie's approach to Human Design is intensely passionate and deeply somatic. She has given some of the best and most accessible advice I've come across when it comes to understanding Sacral authority, which is important for all Generator types to recognize and trust (even those with Emotional authority). Maggie also offers [paid readings ](https://sovereigntybydesign.com/readings/) and runs [Sovereign Sisterhood](https://sovereigntybydesign.com/sovereignsisterhood/), an interactive supportive paid community of women navigating deconditioning and other life transitions. I participated in a free workshop she hosted which explored better accessing somatic decision making through the lens of nervous system regulation, which was a valuable experience and is one that she explores in more depth within her community.

> [!info]- [Christie Inge](https://christieinge.com/) *(website currently undergoing upheaval)*
>  Christie Inge has an *enormous* archive of blog posts, though she has recently announced an intention to rework her library from the ground up, so the best I can say about her site at the moment is "watch this space." Her blog posts certainly apply an experiential lens to the topics she writes about, but the content comfortably straddles the line between "understanding the basics" and diving pretty deep into the mechanics.
> 
>  Christie's posts all contain a disclaimer at the top, warning readers that her content is her personal take on HD and often does not align with the more "traditional" HD perspectives. This is exactly what I like about her posts: the wider the range of perspectives on the same topic that you take in, the more you can see where things overlap, as well as how the same concepts can land very differently in different people. Perspectives from people like Christie Inge, who aren't afraid to challenge a concept that feels rigid, help me to lean into my own inclination to learn by poking holes in things.
> 
>  At the moment, her site contains written posts covering all sorts of things, from [the basics of the system](https://christieinge.com/human-design-101/), key chart features like the [incarnation cross](https://christieinge.com/human-design-incarnation-cross/), nuanced looks at each of the gates, and a [great guide to circuitry](https://christieinge.com/human-design-circuits-101-what-they-are-and-why-they-matter/), all the way to practical guides on [developing emotional intelligence](https://christieinge.com/feel-your-feelings/) and [learning to recognise your own inner wisdom](https://christieinge.com/the-most-important-energy-healing-practice-no-matter-your-human-design/). She also has a great series of posts on the [Projector type, projected channels and gates, projection fields, and psychological projection](https://christieinge.com/human-design-projection-series/). These links may go down at any time at her discretion, so if you're curious I'd recommend taking a look sooner rather than later!
> 
>  Her own chart generator also currently gives you a personalized guide through the lens of your [Gifts & Messages](https://christieinge.com/gifts/), which offers a very empowering perspective on both the defined and undefined centers in your chart.

#### Understanding the Mechanics: for the insatiably curious

> [!info]- [bodygraph.io](https://bodygraph.io/?r=2ATPFRF) *(joining through this link earns me credits towards account perks)*
> Bodygraph.io has recently expanded into a pretty full-service user experience, and is evolving all the time. While there is a knowledge base baked into the site, where I feel it really excels is in clarifying how planetary movements have impact on a chart, and [how they are translated into a chart in the first place](https://bodygraph.io/how-to-read-human-design-chart). This a tool I would have *loved* to have when I was first learning about Human Design.
> 
>  The site has two paid tiers on top of its free user tier, each catering to a different core use. The free tier is aimed at casual users: you can store up to 9 charts, access basic HD knowledge, see which gates are defined by the planets in real time and how those transits affect any of your saved charts, and explore some more unique and interactive ways to understand a chart in the Study Tools section. It also has some community integration, boasting a juicy collection of data and statistics from anonymized contributors, as well as some unique features like emotional wave tracking, opt-in user world map, and site-wide surveys. One thing the free tier offers that I haven't reliably found anywhere else is a mandala view of the chart – essentially a combination of the HD bodygraph and a conventional astrology chart wheel – which could help things click into place for you if you're already familiar with astrology.
> 
>  I would say that the knowledge base at bodygraph.io leans a bit more generic than experiential, which for me just means I deepen my knowledge in other ways. However, this site is my top choice for chart viewing, with the caveat that many of the features I find invaluable are only available at the paid Student tier (side-by-side chart viewing, colour-coded circuitry, and the extremely cool Time Explorer feature which lets you see how a chart changes when the birth time is moved through the course of a day).
> 
>  Talis, who runs bodygraph.io, offers [personalized readings](https://bodygraph.io/app/readings) and is a prolific teacher whose [video lectures](https://bodygraph.io/app/lectures) are available for preview and purchase on the site. His [extensive library of both free and paid Substack articles](https://bodygraph.io/app/articles) can also be browsed at bodygraph.io. 

> [!info]- [Humdes.com](https://www.humdes.com/en/)
> Humdes.com is an informational site that lets free users generate and store multiple charts (I'm not sure what the cap on this is for free users). It has a pretty thorough knowledge base which can be explored both on a per-chart level and as standalone information about the system. It also incorporates concepts of [Gene Keys](https://genekeys.com/free-profile/), a related but standalone system to Human Design, which is interesting in its own right.
> 
> While the depth and breadth of information on humdes.com feels deeply satisfying to me, I find myself remembering the value of holding it all lightly: the presentation makes it a little too tempting to treat all of the personalized information as hard facts, which can be a very mind-forward approach and is not at all what Human Design is really about.

> [!info]- [Evolutionary Human Design](https://evolutionaryhumandesign.com/)
> Evolutionary Human Design is actually the name for a particular approach to Human Design, highlighting practical applications of the system, and [the website is primarily a hub for readings, trainings, books, and coaching](https://evolutionaryhumandesign.com/about/) from both Chetan Parkyn and Carola Eastwood.
> 
> I ended up consulting the Evolutionary Human Design site a *lot* in my early learning because I was especially interested in getting a feel for the gates and lines. You can read [blog posts on each line of every gate in the chart](https://evolutionaryhumandesign.com/category/human-design/book-of-lines/) derived from Chetan Parkyn's book, The Book of Lines, which explores the hexagrams of the Chinese I'Ching (which is where the gates come from) and the nuanced way they can be expressed in each of the six line "flavours." Each page also has an embedded Youtube video where Chetan talks about these in a bit more detail to the written version.
> 
> Overall, what I have mostly gained from studying the gates in this way is a more coherent concept of the six lines and what they're all about: you begin to see how each line has a strong theme that shines through in 64 different ways, and it's really helped me gain an understanding of the lines as they are lived through the Profile of a chart.
