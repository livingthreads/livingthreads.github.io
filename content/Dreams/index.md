# A Primer on Dream Interpretation
Right away, I'll say that the framework I use for dream interpretation comes from the method taught by [Jane Teresa Anderson](https://www.janeteresa.com/about/about_jane_teresa/). She really knows what she's talking about, and if you are at all interested in [the role dreams play in our lives](https://www.janeteresa.com/about/about-dreams/), I encourage you to check out her work:
- [The Dream Show](https://www.janeteresa.com/category/podcasts/), her long-running podcast in which she goes through a dream in real time with a guest
- Her [many books](https://www.janeteresa.com/books/) on the subject
- Her [blog](https://www.janeteresa.com/category/blog/), which is filled with insightful reflections on dreams and dreaming
- Her [teaching courses](https://www.janeteresa.com/online-dream-interpretation-and-dream-therapy-courses/), for those interested in learning to interpret their own dreams

Jane Teresa's signature work, which is applied after interpretation of a dream, is called Dream Alchemy: it's a method she's developed to intentionally spark a change in the unconscious limiting beliefs that a dream interpretation can identify, by working with the symbols of the dream in various ways. It's a powerful tool and I've engaged with it in the past; however, other than using my own improvised variation on this method with my [[Hot Dog Body|childhood dream]], Dream Alchemy doesn't feature in this project's dream journey. While I do think my improvised use of alchemy helped to kickstart my internal shift, I was more interested in observing my inner landscape as it changed than in guiding it consciously (a decision that, in hindsight, I believe is *very* aligned with [[My Human Design Chart#My Chart|my design]]).

Overall, I'd say that my own interest in dream interpretation is much more rooted in observation than application. Why do I interpret my dreams? It's the same reason I study Human Design: I'm interested in the inner workings of the human experience, and particularly in what becomes possible when those inner workings are met with compassionate equanimity. For the time being, that's all I really need it to be about.

### Principles of Interpretation

> [!check] A Helpful List of Guidelines
> When reading through my reflections on my interpretations, it may be helpful to know the basic ideas behind the method I'm using:
> 1. **A dream is a reflection of your inner world.**
>    My take on dreaming is very grounded: rather than seeing dreams as wish fulfilment, prognostication, or spiritual download, I simply look at them as a mirror of the way my mind is processing my life at any given moment (the rule of thumb is that a dream processes the last 24-48 hours, though I've noticed my personal window often tends to reach slightly further back). Our inner landscape is constantly shifting, simply from engaging with life, and to look at our dreams is to get a glimpse of our unconscious workings by interpreting the unconscious mind's fluent language of symbolism into plain language that the conscious mind tends to find more relatable.
> 2. **Everything and everyone in the dream represents something about the dreamer.**
>    Interpreting symbols, even very common ones, is a highly individualized process. The same symbol can mean very different things for different people. Some symbolism might feel immediately obvious to you, but much of it won't. There are many methods to understand the role of an object, creature, person, or setting in your dream. For me, this often comes down to letting my mind wander while reflectively journalling: when my writing lands on something that resonates, I can *feel* the truth of it and suddenly that piece of the dream makes sense.
> 3. **The dynamics between symbols and the movement within the dream story are the heart of the interpretation.**
>    The storytelling aspect of a dream is especially revealing and arguably, it can be found even in the most seemingly mundane snippets of dreams. Thinking about what is actually happening in a dream, both on a broad level and in a more nuanced way, will bring clarity to the nature of the situation your mind is processing.
> 4. **The emotional texture of a dream is almost more important than the symbolism itself.**
>    This one is my own take and quite possibly is just especially true for me personally, though I'd be interested to hear if it resonates for others. Not only are dream emotions *extremely* helpful for linking dream material with waking-life scenarios, but they are excellent at:
>    • *identifying dreams that **want** to be interpreted* (big emotions in dreams = something major is happening inside and is begging for attention)
>    ​• *providing nuance to dream scenarios* (compare a readthrough of my [[Hot Dog Body]] dream on its own to the interpretation of it, where I mention the fear I felt specifically at seeing my insides spill out: this detail was *key* to the full interpretation landing for me)
>    ​• *identifying the **live thread** of a dream* that might feel convoluted and bursting with symbolism. This is my own signature move: my practice of interpretation has, in the past year, gradually become more attuned to picking out this thread (both in my own dreams and in others') in a less-is-more approach to interpretation that chooses to let symbol exploration take a secondary role. Emotional texture often points the way to this thread, although I would define the process of its identification as more intuitive overall.
