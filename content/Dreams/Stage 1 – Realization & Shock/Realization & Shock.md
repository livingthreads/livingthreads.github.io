# "Wait... have I been cut off from my own natural way of being since I was a kid?"
#### My Childhood Dream, from around age 8

> [!quote] Hot Dog Body
> I'm lying naked and immobilized on my grandma's kitchen counter. In the dream, I have just realized that my grandma is a witch and I feel scared because I don't understand her motives. She takes a huge meat cleaver and slices my body straight through down the middle, up to my neck. I can look down and see my body cut cleanly in half with no mess of blood or viscera, just flesh-toned, even-textured, steaming "meat" (this reminds me of splitting open a hot dog).

### 1 December 2025

> [!example] My Interpretation: the realization that set off a whole chain reaction
> > [!example]- [[Dreams/#Principles of Interpretation|Notes and Method:]]
> > 1. This dream is psychological for sure. I can't immediately sense its connection to the mechanics of my chart, so my instinct now is to look at this through symbols and tone, which hopefully will help me get a foothold into something more mechanical.
> >	- being naked: exposure, vulnerability
> >	- immobilization/paralysis: helplessness, lack of agency
> >	- both being naked and being immobilized and the feeling of fear: violation
> >	- my grandma: someone I trust, a caretaker, a role model, a positive and reliable fixture in my life
> >	- witch: mysterious, powerful, esoteric, possibly evil
> >	- kitchen: like a laboratory, experimental, creation, transformation, nourishment
> >	- meat cleaver: tool, weapon, for no-nonsense chopping vs. precision
> >	- bisection: two identical halves, symmetry, division, separation
> >	- clean cut: not messy, contained
> >	- even-textured insides: homogenous, moulded, revealing simplicity
> >	- steam: heat escaping, proof of vitality
> >  >
> >
> >2. Now that I've explored these symbols, I can start to see a picture of the kind of situation I must have been processing at the time and I definitely recognize some familiar themes in my life. I'm a little surprised by how closely they reflect themes I've experienced much more recently; I hadn't realized how early in my life these kinds of feelings were shaping my experience. This dream reveals:
> >	- a feeling of violation from feeling forced into vulnerability
> >	- a part of myself that has felt trustworthy, nurturing, and reliable suddenly feeling unknown to me in a way that feels sinister; a sense of betrayal. (I usually interpret with the mindset that everything and everyone in the dream represents a part of the self, but I do find myself wondering here if, since I was a child, this might also represent my relationships to safety and trust in the world around me)
> >	- feeling dispassionately dissected and roughly exposed, no care given to preserving my form or exploring nuances of my inner workings
> >	- a sense of my inner world being revealed as shockingly nondescript and uncomplicated in a way that almost feels like a relief (in the dream I felt horror and dread at seeing my own realiztic insides)
> >
> 
> *So, a story unfolds:*
> My relationship to trust in my own reliable sense of safety is shaken when I feel forced to reveal parts of my inner world before I am ready. There's a sense that the direct and dispassionate way I have felt forced open is directly responsible for the unremarkable and simplistic inner landscape that is revealed. This also feels like a result that I have influenced through my own fear of seeing the real depth of my own insides flow out; a defensive act that protects my deeper truth from being exposed through brute force.
> 
> *More simply:*
> **When I feel pressured to act or self-express before I am ready in a way that feels impossible to avoid, I learn to only display a bland version of myself that feels passable enough to others that they stop pushing, which leaves my depth feeling safely contained.**
> 
> ---
>
> After interpreting, I reflected a bit on how this kind of internal decision to contain my depth actually did play out through my childhood years. What really struck me was that there wasn't just one effect, but a chain reaction of them. While this strategy of avoiding attention by blending in helped me to feel safe from being forced to participate at a time in my life when I had little agency, it also led to:
> - self-monitoring behaviour 
> ↳ overthinking in-the-moment actions, dulling my connection to embodied decision-making
> ↳ dwelling on missed aligned opportunities
> ↳ feeling like my "inner self" is being consistently unrecognised by others
> ↳ learning to curate my expression to avoid being misunderstood
> ↳ relying on emotional overaccommodation to secure connections

> [!summary]- My thoughts on "Hot Dog Body"
> I had this dream so long ago that it's impossible to know exactly what triggered it, but after I practiced interpreting (primarily looking at the symbolism, but also taking into account the feelings I had within the dream) and read back what I'd written, I had a pretty good guess at what might have happened shortly beforehand.
> 
> When I was a kid, I was enrolled in many different extracurricular classes and workshops. They were always things I'd shown some interest in, but they kind of just happened to me and became a neutral part of my life. My grandma was usually the one who signed me up, took me to lessons, and in the case of piano, made sure I practiced.
> 
> I have a particular memory of being surprised with an enrolment to a kids' choral group, or maybe even a musical theatre day camp, during a school break. It really caught me off guard when my grandma told me I'd been signed up, and I learned that my dad had overheard me singing to myself in my room and had told my grandma that I had a nice singing voice. Being signed up for the camp was meant to be a gift of a surprise, but instead I felt violated. I remember my grandma taking me to each session and I think she even stayed and watched until it was time to go home. I felt trapped there and under so much pressure that I found I couldn't bring myself to sing a single note: I mouthed all the words.
> 
> There was a solo that we could choose to audition for and we were given an accompaniment tape to take home so we could practice. I was curious about what soloing might be like, even though I hadn't been doing any singing with the group. I remember shutting myself in my room with my tape deck when I felt sure that nobody else in the house was nearby, and singing the solo so faintly, absolutely terrified of being overheard.
> 
> With very few exceptions (drowned out at a concert, one particularly fun time with a small group while drunk), I have never earnestly sung in front of anybody since: any time when I would have been expected to sing, I have either faked it, put on a goofy voice, or made an excuse not to, feeling that same terror I had alone in my bedroom while practicing the solo.
> 
> I hold no ill will towards my family members for this memorable incident and never have: it was obviously not malicious and simply felt like another thing that kind of just happened. I have been consciously aware that it marked some kind of change in me but have blithely stored that knowledge in the back of my head, like "haha maybe one day I'll dig that up with a therapist." However, revisiting it through the context of this dream and seeing how my interpretation suggested it might actually have affected me over time came as a complete surprise.
> 
> I've written a separate post on [[HD Themes in my Childhood Dream#The parts of my chart that " Hot Dog Body " highlighted|the Human Design angle]], for those curious.

### Insight leading to compassion
I had chosen this dream to practice interpretation on simply because childhood dreams often have particularly vivid symbolism and because this one was memorable; I certainly didn't expect to touch on such a significant turning point in my life. The realization had that dull thud of incontrovertible truth through insight, a moment that for me is a simple internal "oh."

What's interesting is that I've had an awareness of this kind of self-inhibiting behaviour for a very long time and I've always felt that it somehow made me "broken," but I truly was shocked to realize through my interpretation that ***I*** was the one who created the split in me between my complex authentic self and the way I interact with the world: an external force (the adults in my life pushing participation on my behalf) may have been the trigger, but keeping myself contained was my own response. It wasn't a wound so much as a protective strategy, and suddenly being able to see it that way made that feeling of brokenness dissolve.

> [!check] Working with the dream: Improvised Alchemy
> I felt such a deep compassion for my younger self in that realization and I decided I wanted to work with the dream symbols, both to put the violence of the dream to rest, and to begin to find a way forward from acknowledging what I'd endured. I did this through a sort of makeshift adaptation of a process called Dream Alchemy (coined by dream analyst [Jane Teresa Anderson](https://www.janeteresa.com/)): essentially, using the symbols from the dream to visualize a resolution that supports integration.
> 
> Recognising that this had been with me for so long, and that I had built an internal framework to navigate around that split, I think I knew that totally healing or reversing the cut wasn't an option: instead, I worked with the symbols to stitch the cut closed as a decoration. The ultimate goal was to let my young self work on her own body at her own pace, giving her agency where she'd had none before.
> 
> I visualized accompanying her in the empty kitchen and in place of the cleaver, I provided her with a mystical golden thread. Then I just let my imagination show me what happened next: she used it to pull the halves of her body together, joining completely at evenly-spaced points while leaving regular gaps along the seam, the edges of the open flesh adorned with gold. I felt then that she wanted to sit in the other room, and after quietly sitting beside her on a couch, I offered her a blanket and sat with her as she went to sleep.
> 
> While having the realization about the weight of the childhood dream was probably shock enough for the rest of the dreams over the next six months to take shape, I think the gentle sort of "aftercare" handling of this dream helped it to really take root; it's like my entire system recognised that *something* big was finally ready to shift. And shift, it did.

# "How do I even know who I am underneath who I learned to be?"
### Night of 2 December 2025

#### Early shock processing after a major internal shift: realizing parts of me that had felt like my own were actually "alien"

> [!quote] Alien Invasion
> I look out my living room window and notice the clouds in the sky becoming "angry" in a way that feels interesting and unusual. I can only see the edges of the clouds from the window because they're directly overhead, but I point out to my partner that there must be an incredible storm brewing because the clouds are dark and are moving around dramatically, like wiggling fingers.
> 
> I look away for a moment and when I look back out the window, the scene has rapidly progressed: there is a huge, dark, indigo-black twister bearing down surprisingly close. I scream in terror. It's like I can't scream enough. At first I'm scared just because I find twisters and tornadoes scary (I've never seen one before and I hope I never will), but then it gets even scarier because I start picking up on subtle signs that tell me the twister isn't a natural phenomenon. I realize it's a manifestation of an alien takeover or abduction situation.
> 
> I can't remember a lot of details about the next parts, but I had many smaller dream fragments that took place within this alien takeover scenario:
> - The aliens had sent small surveillance robots to the planet's surface and being spotted by one could transport a person away, presumably to their ship, on sight. I was evading cameras and taking down the robots in a way that felt like a fierce and protective resistance.
> - I was preparing a lunch under duress, picking out convenience foods at a grocery store to pair together as a meal to be presented. This whole part felt tense, rushed, and crucial, like my life could depend on it. I remember tears on my face and my hands shaking as I tried to open a packet of ham slices.
> - I remember urgently trying to close a window behind a closed curtain, but the wind keeps sticking the curtains against the screen and they get in the way.
> - Something about building a tall tower of objects outside as a test or tribute or offering.

> [!summary]- My thoughts on "Alien Invasion"
> This entire night of dreams felt unstoppably frightening and ungrounded, but what was really happening here was enormously reflective. First a forceful upheaval (tornado) reveals an alien presence. Then old scenarios similar to those in my childhood dream start playing out under that heading:
> - The window that I can't close echoes the feeling of not being able to hold a boundary that feels crucial
> - Preparing the high-stakes lunch mirrors feeling forced to perform under pressure
> 
> Both of these pieces were giving me a new way to look at these familiar behaviours as being shaped by something outside of myself, and primarily about survival: I'd have rather closed the window and would rather not have made a lunch to be judged, but the alien presence (I'm including the wind here) eliminated my sense of agency.
> 
> The surveillance robot piece has a different flavour. Even though the fear in this part of the dream was present, I was willing and able to evade and fight against these vigilant agents working for the alien presence. The resistance here felt important, very "this hypervigilant presence doesn't get to decide for me." It was me making a choice to fight for my autonomy despite a heavy invading presence (in this case, my own learned hypervigilance) that sought to take it away.
> 
> The building of the tower was interesting and a little less obvious to me. I was collecting objects and stacking them somewhat experimentally, even in the dream not quite sure if I was doing it right or what I was aiming for, but knowing that it was being judged and scored. The feeling was less about an existential fear of failing (like with the lunch prep) and more about uncertainty and having no idea which choices would earn or lose me points. It felt important in a different way than the other pieces, and if I had to sum it up in a sentence, it might be: "is what I've collected here enough for me to progress?"
> 
> Looking back at the nature of this night of dreams and knowing where I was headed in the nights to come makes me think building the tower was a simple self-test of initiation: had I gotten what I needed from the dream to move on to the next step? Though in the dream I never discovered my final score, I can safely assume in hindsight that undoubtedly, I had passed.
